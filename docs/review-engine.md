# Review Engine: Owner Playbook

Google reviews are the biggest thing we control for ranking in the Google Maps "local pack." The goal is a
**steady weekly trickle** of honest reviews, started now and never stopped.

Live profile as of 2026-09-26: **5.0 stars from 3 Google reviews.**

## After every job: 3 steps, then stop

1. **Ask in person at handoff.** When you walk the owner or manager through the finished property (or send the
   completion photos), ask directly: *"If you were happy with today, a Google review would really help us. Can I send
   you the link?"* A personal ask, face to face, is what makes the follow-up link get used.
2. **Send the link within 24 hours.** Use the text or email template below while the clean is still fresh.
3. **One reminder on day 5.** Only if they have not posted. Then **stop.** No third message, ever.

Ask each customer **once**. For recurring hosts and property managers, ask once after the first few jobs go well,
not after every turnover.

## Pace: a trickle, never a batch

- Ask as jobs happen. A few new reviews every week beats twenty in one weekend.
- **Never** save up a customer list and send it all at once, and never run a "review drive." Bursts look
  inorganic, and Google can filter or remove reviews that arrive in a spike.
- Google rules (breaking them can get reviews removed or the profile suspended):
  - No discounts, freebies, or anything else in exchange for a review.
  - Ask **every** customer the same way. Do not pick only the happy ones or screen people first.
  - No reviews from staff, family, or friends, and never write or post a review for a customer on their device.

## Reply to every review within 48 hours

- **Every** review gets a reply, including a bare star rating with no text. Google and future customers both see it.
- Thank them by first name, mention the service or town naturally ("our New Smyrna Beach turnover team"), and keep
  it to two or three sentences. Never include guest names, door codes, or other private details.
- **Negative review:** stay calm, apologize for the experience, do not argue the facts in public, and take it
  offline: "Please call us at (386) 301-5775 so we can make this right." A professional reply to a bad review
  persuades more readers than the bad review scares off.

## Google Business Profile: still on the owner

Check these off in the Google Business Profile dashboard:

- [ ] **Primary category: "House cleaning service"**, not the generic "Cleaners." Local SEO practitioners
      consistently rank the primary category as one of the strongest signals on the profile.
- [ ] **Secondary categories:** in the category picker, search "vacation," "rental," and "cleaning" and add any
      that match work we actually do (vacation rental or short-term rental cleaning style categories, if Google
      offers them). Only add categories for real services.
- [ ] **Hours: confirm which is right.** Google shows "Opens 7 AM"; the website says **8:00 AM to 6:00 PM, Mon to
      Sun**, plus the 24/7 emergency line. Pick one and tell the web team so both say the same thing.
- [ ] **20+ photos from real jobs:** finished rooms, before/after pairs, towel art, the team at work. Add a few new
      ones every month; no stock photos.
- [ ] **Seed 5 to 8 Q&A:** ask and answer the questions customers really ask (turnover time, same-day and
      emergency service, supplies and linens, service area, how quotes work, insurance).
- [ ] **Services list with the turnover price tiers:** Studio/1BR $100 to $140, 2BR $140 to $200, 3BR+ $200 to
      $280. Property management volume pricing: 10% off for 3 to 5 properties, 15% off plus a dedicated manager for
      6 to 15, custom for 16+. Residential, deep, and eco-friendly cleaning: "custom quote within 24 hours."
- [ ] **Service area: the seven cities:** Daytona Beach, Daytona Beach Shores, Ormond Beach, Ormond-by-the-Sea,
      New Smyrna Beach, Port Orange, Ponce Inlet. Keep the street address hidden (service-area business).
- [ ] **Post weekly:** a finished-job photo, a seasonal tip (race week, snowbird season, hurricane prep), or an
      availability note. One short post a week keeps the profile active.

## How we measure it

Every click on a review link **on the website** (the footer star, the home page testimonial badge, and the two
links under the testimonials) is logged in Google Analytics as the event **`review_click`**, with the link that was
clicked. People who type the short link from a printed card go straight to Google without loading a page, so they
do not show up there. Once a month, write down the review count and star rating on the profile; that number is the
real scoreboard.
